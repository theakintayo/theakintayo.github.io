Zainab AKINTAYO

MS student in Engineering Artificial Intelligence, Carnegie Mellon University Africa

Carnegie Mellon University Africa

Kigali, Rwanda

Zainab Akintayo is a Master’s student in Engineering Artificial Intelligence at Carnegie Mellon University Africa, with a background in Civil Engineering from Ladoke Akintola University of Technology, Nigeria, where she graduated with First Class Honours as the best graduating student. Her research interests lie at the intersection of artificial intelligence, environmental engineering, and computational materials science, with a particular focus on developing intelligent, data-driven solutions for sustainable water treatment and environmental systems.

Her research explores the application of machine learning, digital twins, and molecular simulations to understand, predict, and optimize complex physical and chemical processes. Her work includes developing data-driven digital twins for coagulant dosing optimization in drinking water treatment, investigating coagulant–pollutant interactions using molecular docking and density functional theory (DFT), and applying computational methods to evaluate materials for water purification. She is particularly interested in integrating mechanistic understanding with uncertainty-aware and interpretable AI to improve the reliability, adaptability, and practical deployment of intelligent water treatment systems.

Through her research, Zainab aims to bridge the gap between computational modeling and real-world engineering applications, contributing to the development of more efficient, resilient, and sustainable water treatment technologies.

Research interests: smart, AI-enabled water and environmental systems · machine learning for water quality and process optimization · uncertainty-aware decision support · computational chemistry and molecular modeling

  1. LAUJET
    Pozzolanic Potential of Anacardium Occidentale Nutshell Ash (AONSA) and Its Impact on the Mechanical Properties of Concrete
    A. A. Adegbola, S. A. Ayanlere, E. Ibiwoye, and 9 more authors
    LAUTECH Journal of Engineering and Technology, Aug 2025
    Abs Bib HTML PDF

    This study investigates the pozzolanic potential of Anacardium occidentale nutshell ash (AONSA), an agricultural waste, and its influence on the mechanical properties of concrete. Discarded cashew nutshells, commonly dumped without control, pose environmental risks; however, their ash, when properly processed, can serve as a sustainable cement substitute. AONSA was produced through open-air combustion and calcined under two conditions: 800°C for 5 hours (Sample A) and 500°C for 7 hours (Sample B). The samples were characterized using X-Ray Diffraction (XRD), X-Ray Fluorescence (XRF), and Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM). XRD revealed mineral phases including quartz, muscovite, glauconite, and illite. XRF showed substantial contents of SiO2, CaO, and MgO, indicating pozzolanic suitability. SEM analysis revealed microstructural transformation at higher temperatures, with Sample A exhibiting increased porosity and lower potassium content, characteristics favourable for reactivity. Concrete samples incorporating 0–10% AONSA were tested for compressive strength at 7, 14, 28, and 56 days. Results showed that 2% AONSA replacement yielded the highest compressive strength across most curing periods, surpassing the control at 7 days (10.64 vs. 6.31 N/mm2) and maintaining comparable strength at 28 days (17.78 vs. 18.13 N/mm2). However, strength declined beyond 4% replacement. The combination of chemical, microstructural, and mechanical evaluations confirms that AONSA, particularly at lower replacement levels, has strong potential as a pozzolanic material for sustainable concrete applications.
