Water (H2O), a seemingly ordinary compound of two hydrogen atoms and one oxygen atom is the lifeblood of our planet, playing an indispensable role in sustaining all forms of life and as a nearly universal solvent.

Professor Paul Westerhoff whose research on atmospheric water harvesting I enjoy so much once said “Even when you don’t see water, taste water or touch water, you’re still using water”.

The blue gold plays its valuable roles in;

  • Human Bodies: Facilitating various bodily functions, regulates body temperature and maintaining optimal hydration levels.
  • Backbone of Agriculture: Serving as the primary resource for irrigation, sustains crop growth, ensuring food security and flourishing of a vibrant ecosystem.
  • Maintains the delicate balance of the earth’s ecosystem.
  • Source of life for both terrestrial and aquatic organisms.
  • A vital medium for nutrient transport, soil erosion control and temperature regulation.
  • The wetlands (areas of land saturated or flooded with water permanently or seasonally) act as natural filters for the purification and improvement of water quality.
  • A vital resource for various industries and also a potential source of renewable energy.
As valuable as water is, its scarcity is a looming threat on the ecosystem as the number of people with access to water steadily increases.

Although, water makes up over 70% of the Earth’s surface, and 97% of this being ocean water which are salty/saline, the freshwater available for use is just 3% of this composition.

Most of the uneven distribution of freshwater around the world can be explained by the different levels of precipitation (rainfall and snow). However, climate change also has an impact on precipitation with the increase in floods and droughts that affect some areas more than the others.

You now have a glimpse to how much precious water is, so make sure to use water intently like the gold it is, remembering that many regions would cross mountains to possess that blue gold in your hands.
